# Categories, credits and counterpart matching

Use actual household category identifiers and conventions; category labels alone can be ambiguous. Preserve deliberate assignments unless evidence or user context supports a change. Merchant names suggest questions, not purpose. A payment service transfer may be spending, a reimbursement or an internal transfer; ask when the distinction is not evidenced. A single answer can support a one-off classification; a rule requires the separate preview and approval path.

Credit-card payments and household transfers generally move money between accounts rather than create spending or income. Confirm that both accounts are in scope and use the app's supported transfer treatment. A loan payment may contain principal, interest and fees: principal is not automatically an expense, and the total payment does not prove the allocation. Preserve the evidence-backed components.

Refunds ordinarily offset the evidenced original spending category, including split allocations. Reimbursements follow the household's recorded treatment and payer context. Do not force cashback, reversals, rewards, store credit or unrelated credits into the refund model. Use [refunds and reimbursements](refunds-and-reimbursements.md) for many-record relationships and expected-credit follow-through. An original purchase outside the requested period may be read for context without authorizing an edit.

Distinguish **evidence matched** from **linked in app**. Along can track an existing relationship locally when native linking is absent or unsafe. A native link needs an observed capability, an exact approved scope and verification of every affected record; it must not create an unapproved counterpart, move source dates or change unrelated fields. In a mixed-ownership pair, the stricter account's operation permission governs. Never count a transfer twice as spending.

Reconcile each cross-currency side in its own currency and retain observed exchange, fee and settlement evidence. Equal converted values are not proof. If fees, grouping, timing or foreign exchange prevent a confident match, report an unresolved candidate rather than forcing double entry.
